# Trust Accounting in NSW - Practical Compliance Guide

Law stated as at 8 September 2026.

## 1. How to use this guide

This is a working compliance reference: what you must do, by when, and under which provision. Every requirement carries a hyperlinked citation to the NSW legislation register. Where a requirement comes from the Law Society's interpretation rather than the legislation, it is labelled *Law Society view*.

### 1.2 Three things that changed after the course material was written

| Change | When | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Application Regulation 2015 repealed and remade as the 2025 Regulation | 1 Sep 2025 | Statutory deposit provisions moved from cll 9-13 to ss 10-15 |
| *Financial Transaction Reports Act 1988* (Cth) repealed | 7 Jan 2025 | Solicitors' $10,000 cash reports under that Act ceased |
| AML/CTF Tranche 2 commenced for law practices | 1 Jul 2026 | Law practices providing designated services are AUSTRAC reporting entities; cash reporting is now by threshold transaction report |

The course slide deck also ran the principal's **responsibility** provision and the principal's **liability** provision together under one heading. They are separate sections and are separated at section 3 below.

### 1.3 The two numbers that govern everything

- **5 working days** to record a receipt, payment or transfer in the cash books and the trust ledger.
- **15 working days after month end** for the bank reconciliation, the trial balance and the controlled money listing.

Start the reconciliation on the first working day of the new month, not the fourteenth. The reconciliation is what surfaces errors, and errors compound.

- **NSW law practices**: the Law Society of NSW ([Application Act s 11](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-016#sec.11)).

## 3. Who is responsible

### 3.1 Section 34 - responsibilities of principals

Each principal must ensure reasonable steps are taken so that (a) all legal practitioner associates comply with their obligations under the Uniform Law, the Uniform Rules and their other professional obligations, and (b) legal services provided by the practice are provided in accordance with those obligations ([s 34](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.34)).

Failure may amount to unsatisfactory professional conduct or professional misconduct ([ss 296](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.296), [297](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.297), [298](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.298)).

### 3.2 Section 35 - liability of principals

If the practice contravenes an obligation imposed on it, a principal is **taken to have contravened the same provision** if the principal knowingly authorised or permitted it, **or** was in (or ought reasonably to have been in) a position to influence the practice's conduct and failed to take reasonable steps to prevent it ([s 35(1)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.35)). That contravention is itself capable of being unsatisfactory professional conduct or professional misconduct ([s 35(2)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.35)).

The second limb is the operative one. You do not need to have known.

A solicitor with designated responsibility for a matter must exercise reasonable supervision over everyone providing legal services on it ([ASCR r 37](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0244#sec.37)).

### 3.3 What "reasonable steps" means in practice

Four obligations, drawn from ss 34 and 35 and the record-keeping provisions:

1. **A proper system** of accounting and file management exists. Any coherent system will do; chaos will not. Compliant software is expensive at start-up but prompts you through the steps.
2. **You can access it.** If an external bookkeeper maintains the records, you have your own login and can look at any time.
3. **The legislation is complied with.**
4. **You personally check.** Not the bookkeeper telling you it is fine. You look at the records, particularly after any suspected problem, to verify remediation yourself.

### 3.4 The partner's duty - settled authority

The disciplinary and appellate authority is consistent and has not softened. The operative principles:

- A partner cannot answer a breach by saying the financial affairs were left to another partner, and certainly not where warnings had been received: *Re Mayes and Legal Practitioners Act* [1974] 1 NSWLR 19.
- Feelings of delicacy, or a belief that queries would offend the spirit of mutual trust between partners, do not excuse abdication: *Mayes*.
- Each partner must see there is a proper system to which each has access, see the rules are obeyed, and check the system. **The existence of a system does not relieve partners of personal vigilance**: *Mayes*.
- Where a partner knows gross breaches have occurred, verbal assurances from other partners are not enough. **The partner has to look**: *Bridges v Law Society of New South Wales* [1983] 2 NSWLR 361.
- The extent of involvement varies with the size of the practice and its organisation. Partners should regard themselves as a group collectively determined to safeguard the trust account, and should actively seek oversight of their own trust dealings by other partners. Requiring **two partners to sign any trust cheque** is a useful safeguard. Where such safeguards are absent, periodic inspection of the trust ledger by each partner in turn is the only remaining control: *Somers*, Supreme Court of the ACT, No SC1912 of 1978.
- Where irregularities are drawn to a partner's attention, failure to enquire and report is itself misconduct: *Matter of James Lloyd Hutchison*, Legal Profession Disciplinary Reports No 1, 1993.
- But where nothing on the face of properly constituted records or the system itself should have raised suspicion, a passive partner may not be liable: *Matter No 15 of 1986*, Solicitors' Statutory Committee.

**Practical rule.** Personal checks are limited to what comes to your attention, until something raises a question about the system. From that moment you must enquire until personally satisfied the system is effective.

### 3.5 Firm culture

If the partner responsible for trust resists scrutiny, that is itself the risk. Asking questions about trust, or examining the records yourself, must carry no social cost.

## 4. Identifying trust money

### 4.1 Definition

Money entrusted to a law practice in the course of or in connection with the provision of legal services, including money received on account of legal costs in advance, controlled money, transit money, and money subject of a power ([s 129(1)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.129)).

**Three elements**: money; entrusted to a law practice; in the course of or in connection with the provision of legal services.

### 4.2 When money is received

When the practice obtains possession or control directly, or indirectly through delivery to an associate, or when the practice or an associate is given a power or authority to deal with money for another ([s 153(1)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.153)). Money is taken to be received where it is made available by EFT, credit card or telegraphic transfer ([s 153(2)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.153)).

Trust money may be dealt with only by the practice or an associate ([s 135(2)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.135)).

### 4.3 Common calls

| Situation | Answer |
|---|---|
| Retainer paid monthly for availability | **Trust money** until the services are performed. Draw under rule 42 once earned |
| Money up front for disbursements on a conveyance | **Trust money** |
| Money up front for litigation | **Trust money** |
| Money for services already provided, bill given | **Not trust money** ([s 129(2)(a)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.129)). Draw it out promptly |
| Client asks you to hold money unconnected with any legal service | **Not trust money**, no fidelity fund protection. Consider whether to hold it at all |

### 4.4 What is not trust money, and when a notice is required

| Provision | Excluded money | s 134 notice? |
|---|---|---|
| [s 129(2)(a)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.129) | Services already provided, bill given | No |
| [s 129(2)(b)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.129) | Managed investment scheme or mortgage financing undertaken by the practice | Yes |
| [s 129(2)(c)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.129) | Financial service requiring an AFSL, or provided as a representative of a financial services business | Yes |
| [s 129(2)(d)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.129) | Investment money failing the two-limb test below | Yes |
| [s 129(2)(e)-(f)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.129) | Money determined under s 152 or declared by the Uniform Rules not to be trust money | Yes |

**The s 134 notice.** Where the practice receives or holds non-trust money (other than money for legal costs due to it), it must give the provider **written notice** that the money will not be treated as trust money, is not subject to the trust money provisions, and cannot found a fidelity fund claim ([s 134](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.134)).

### 4.5 Investment money that *is* trust money

Money received for investment purposes is trust money only if **both** limbs are met ([s 129(2)(d)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.129)):

- received in the ordinary course of legal practice **and** primarily in connection with the provision of legal services at the client's direction; **and**
- the investment is made in the ordinary course of legal practice **and** for the ancillary purpose of maintaining or enhancing the value of the money or property.

*Law Society view:* obtain a written direction from the person on whose behalf the money is held (unless acting under a power), to evidence that the practice is not making investment decisions and risking breach of the *Corporations Act 2001* (Cth).

### 4.6 Authority to receive trust money

A practice must not receive trust money unless a principal holds a practising certificate authorising the receipt of trust money, or the practice is otherwise authorised under the Uniform Rules ([s 150](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.150)). Practising certificates carry a condition as to whether the holder may receive trust money ([s 47(2)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.47)).

*Law Society view:* where the practice has a principal so authorised, solicitor and non-solicitor associates may receive trust money on the practice's behalf and account to it.

### 4.7 Decision sequence for each receipt

1. Entrusted in connection with legal services? If no, not trust money.
2. Services already provided and billed? Not trust money ([s 129(2)(a)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.129)).
3. Financial service requiring an AFSL? Not trust money; give a s 134 notice.
4. **Is it cash?** It must go to the GTA (or CMA for controlled money) before anything else, whatever the direction says ([s 143](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.143)). Then continue.
5. Instructions to pay or deliver to a third party who is not an associate? **Transit money** ([s 140](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.140)).
6. Written direction to deposit into a non-GTA account under the practice's exclusive control? **Controlled money** ([s 139](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.139)).
7. Written direction from a person legally entitled to give it, to deal with the money otherwise than by depositing to the GTA? **Written direction money** ([s 137(a)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.137); [r 34](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.34)).
8. Subject to a power to receive or disburse for another? **Power money** ([s 141](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.141)).
9. Otherwise **general trust money** ([ss 137](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.137), [138(1)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.138)).

## 5. The five categories at a glance

| | What triggers it | What you must do | Records | Statement required? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **General trust money** | Default position | Deposit to the GTA at an authorised ADI in NSW as soon as practicable ([s 137](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.137), [s 136](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.136)). Hold exclusively for the person; disburse only on their direction ([s 138](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.138)) | rr 36-49 | Yes |
| **Controlled money** | Written direction to deposit into a non-GTA account under the practice's exclusive control | Deposit as soon as practicable into the account specified. No pooling ([s 139](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.139)) | rr 61-64 | Yes |
| **Transit money** | Instructions to pay or deliver to a third party who is not an associate | Pay or deliver within the period specified, else as soon as practicable ([s 140(1)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.140)) | Brief particulars, 7 years ([s 140(2)-(3)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.140)) | **No** |
| **Written direction money** | Written direction from a person legally entitled, to deal otherwise than by GTA deposit | Comply within the period specified, else as soon as practicable ([s 142](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.142)) | Keep the direction 7 years | **No** |
| **Power money** | Power or authority to deal with money for another (POA, probate, guardianship, account signing authority) | Deal only in accordance with the power ([s 141](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.141)) | rr 55, 60 | Yes |
| **Investment of trust money** | Direction to invest, meeting the s 129(2)(d) test | Invest per the direction | r 59 | Yes |

### 5.1 The cash rule

**All trust money received as cash goes into the GTA first** (or the CMA, for controlled money), before being dealt with in any other way, regardless of anything to the contrary in the direction or instructions ([s 143](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.143)).

Practical handling: discourage large cash tenders. You assume responsibility from the moment it is handed over and someone must physically bank it. Better to send the payer to the bank to deposit directly into your trust account, then issue a receipt on confirmation.

**AML overlay.** Since 1 July 2026, law practices providing designated services are reporting entities under the [AML/CTF Act 2006 (Cth)](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2006A00169/latest/text) and cash is reported by threshold transaction report. The handout's reference to a Form 15A under the repealed *Financial Transaction Reports Act 1988* is superseded. See the **Anti-Money Laundering** guide.

### 5.2 Points that are commonly got wrong

**Transit money** is a third-party cheque passing across your desk (classically, a duty cheque payable to Revenue NSW). If the money is paid to you and goes into your trust account, it is not transit money. Keep a photocopy of the cheque plus any settlement directions on the file.

**Written direction money** cannot be directed into your office account or any account holding other money, because [s 146](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.146) forbids intermixing. "Legally entitled" persons include a spouse with a Family Court order, a trustee in bankruptcy with a sequestration order, a guardian with a guardianship order, or a government agency with a statutory notice. Satisfy yourself that the entitlement covers the specific money.

**Power money** costs cannot be drawn under rule 42. Costs can be taken only under the power itself.

**Controlled money** exists so the **client** earns the interest. Interest on the GTA goes to the Public Purpose Fund. For any substantial sum held for any period, raise a CMA with the client. If you hold trust money for only one client, a CMA avoids the machinery of a GTA entirely.

## 6. Opening and running a general trust account

### 6.1 Establishment (r 35)

Provision: [r 35](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.35)

- With an **authorised** ADI (not merely an ADI). List on the [Law Society site](https://www.lawsociety.com.au/practising-law-in-NSW/trust-money-and-fidelity-funds/authorised-deposit-taking-institutions); the general ADI list is on the [APRA site](https://www.apra.gov.au/industries/authorised-deposit-taking-institutions).
- Maintained **in NSW** ([s 136](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.136)).
- Account name must include the practice name or business name **and** the expression "law practice trust account" or "law practice trust a/c".

Contact the bank in advance and deal with someone senior. Branch staff routinely do not understand solicitors' trust accounts or statutory deposit accounts, and opening the wrong account type is a recurring problem.

Forms: [Law Society notification forms](https://www.lawsociety.com.au/practising-law-in-NSW/trust-money-and-fidelity-fund/notification-forms).

Branch trust records may be kept at the branch if the Law Society is notified ([s 136(2)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.136)).

### 6.3 The four absolute prohibitions

| Prohibition | Provision |
|---|---|
| **No intermixing.** Trust money must not be mixed with other money, in either direction, except as authorised by the DLRA | [s 146](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.146) |
| **No deficiency.** Must not, without reasonable excuse, cause a deficiency in any trust account or trust ledger, or a failure to pay or deliver trust money. Penalty: 500 penalty units or 5 years, or both | [s 148](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.148) |
| **No false names.** Must not knowingly receive or record money under a false name; must record all names by which a person is known | [s 147(3)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.147) |
| **No borrowing from clients**, or assisting an associate to do so, subject to the rule's exemptions | [ASCR r 12.3](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0244#sec.12) |

Trust money is not available for the practice's debts and is not liable to attachment or execution against the practice or an associate ([s 145](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.145)).

**Intermixing in practice.** The two commonest breaches are leaving billed costs sitting in trust (once billed for services provided, it is your money and no longer trust money), and taking disbursement funds straight into the office account. Also: a credit card payment may be accepted as trust money only if the **whole** amount is credited to the trust account with fees debited to office. You cannot take trust money by credit card to the office account and transfer it across. On a chargeback, the practice must deposit the chargeback amount into the GTA and pursue recovery separately.

### 6.4 Transfer of a practice (Solicitors Rules r 6)

Provision: [Solicitors Rules r 6](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0245#sec.6)

Before delivering possession of the practice, give each client at least **14 days'** written notice of the intended transfer of documents and of their right to direct otherwise. Where trust money is held, the notice must state the balance held, your intention to transfer the account, and the client's right to direct otherwise. Does not apply where a new partner is admitted to a continuing partnership.

The same logic applies internally: an employed solicitor cannot take a file and client with them. The client belongs to the practice.

## 7. The records you must keep

### 7.1 The keeping obligation (s 147)

Provision: [s 147](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.147)

Trust records must be kept in **permanent form** (printed or, on request, capable of being printed, in English on paper or other material), and:

- in a way that **at all times discloses the true position** for each person;
- in a way that enables the records to be **conveniently and properly investigated or externally examined**;
- for **7 years** after the last transaction entry, or finalisation of the matter, whichever is later;
- in accordance with the Uniform Rules.

No pencil. Records kept only in a spreadsheet do not meet permanent form unless printed.

**Trust records** are defined at [s 128](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.128) and include receipts, cheque butts and requisitions, records of EFT authorities, deposit records, trust ADI statements, receipts and payments cash books, trust ledger accounts, monthly trial balances, monthly reconciliations, transfer journals, statements of account, registers, monthly statements, files relating to trust transactions and bills, written directions and authorities, and supporting information for powers.

### 7.2 Trust receipts (r 36)

Provision: [r 36](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.36)

Made out **as soon as practicable** after receipt, including for direct deposits and for withdrawals of statutory deposit back into the GTA. The original is given on request to the payer.

**Form**: duplicate; consecutively numbered and issued in sequence; practice name or business name; the expression "Trust Account" or "Trust A/c".

**Required particulars:**

1. Date the receipt is made out, and the date of receipt of the money if different
2. Receipt number
3. Amount received
4. Form in which received (cash, cheque, direct deposit, credit card)
5. Name of the person from whom received
6. Name of the client in respect of whom received, plus matter description and matter reference
7. Particulars sufficient to identify the reason for the receipt
8. Name of the person who made out the receipt

Record both dates only where they differ. Where the system uses a ledger reference distinct from the matter reference, record both. *Law Society view:* the maker's name must be clearly printed so the person can be identified.

**Duplicates.** A computerised system that permits a receipt entry to be changed after acceptance does not remove the need for a duplicate receipt.

**Cancelled receipts** ([r 36(6)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.36)). Only where the error is found **before issue** and the original is retained. Mark original and duplicate "Cancelled" with the reason, staple together, and enter in the receipts cash book in receipt number sequence with a nil amount. Do not post to a ledger under a manual system. If the receipt has already been issued, do not cancel it; correct by another route.

**Multi receipts.** One receipt covering several matters for one payment is acceptable.

**Dishonoured cheques.** Not a cancellation and no negative receipt. Retain the dishonour notice, rewrite the entry in the receipts cash book as a **negative** amount with the reason, and post to the **debit** side of the ledger. If the receipt has already been drawn against, deposit office funds into the GTA **immediately** to remedy the deficiency, issue a receipt, and **notify under [s 154](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.154)**.

**Cheque clearance.** No prescribed clearance period. Drawing against an uncleared cheque that then dishonours breaches [s 138(1)(a)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.138) and [s 148](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.148). Be guided by your ADI.

### 7.3 Deposit records (r 37)

Provision: [r 37](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.37)

In **duplicate**, produced to the ADI for each deposit other than direct deposits, filed in the order deposits were made.

**Required particulars:** date of deposit; amount of deposit; the cheques, notes and coins content and the amount of each; and for each cheque, the drawer's name, the name and branch or BSB of the ADI on which it is drawn, and the amount.

**Butt-style deposit slips cannot be used** because the drawer and bank details are surrendered to the ADI. Banks often issue them to new account holders; do not use them.

Not required for direct deposits, but confirm the ADI has acknowledged the funds before drawing on them. The date and amount of each deposit must also appear in the receipts cash book ([r 44(2)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.44)).

**Quick banking**: only if your own records would let you reconstruct a lost deposit. Never for deposits including cash. Keep a copy of the deposit slip.

### 7.4 Payments (s 144(1), r 43)

Provisions: [s 144(1)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.144), [r 43](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.43)

**Only two methods: cheque or electronic funds transfer.** No cash withdrawals, no ATM withdrawals or transfers, no telephone withdrawals or transfers. Civil penalty 50 penalty units.

*Practice note:* BPAY and PayID payments out of a trust account are treated as forms of EFT. The rule text refers only to cheque and EFT, so ensure the written record captures the same particulars and the payment is authorised the same way. For **controlled money**, the Law Society's guidance lists BPAY among prohibited withdrawal methods.

**Cheques must**: be payable to or to the order of a specified person and **not to bearer or cash**; be crossed **"not negotiable"**; carry the practice name or business name and the expression "law practice trust account" or "law practice trust a/c"; and be numbered in series. A client cannot direct you to breach this: a cheque to cash leaves no audit trail.

**Written record of each payment** must be kept unless the particulars are recorded by a computerised system in the payments cash book **at the time** the cheque is issued or the transfer is effected; and even then, a written record sufficient to verify the system's accuracy must be kept ([r 43(3)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.43)).

**Cheque record particulars** (kept in cheque number order):

1. Date and number of the cheque
2. Amount ordered to be paid
3. Name of the payee, or if payable to an ADI, the name or BSB of the ADI **and** the name of the person receiving the benefit
4. Name of the person on whose behalf the payment was made, and the matter reference
5. Details clearly identifying the ledger account to be debited
6. Particulars sufficient to identify the reason for the payment

**EFT record particulars:**

1. Date of transfer
2. Reference number or other means of identifying the transfer
3. Amount
4. Name and number of the account transferred to, and the BSB
5. Name of the person on whose behalf the payment was made and the matter reference (or, for an ADI payment, the ADI name or BSB and the beneficiary's name)
6. Details clearly identifying the ledger account to be debited
7. Particulars sufficient to identify the reason for the payment

**EFT reference numbers.** Generate an internal EFT reference in consecutive sequence, enter it in the description field in internet banking, and record it in the payments cash book and ledger like a cheque number. File source records in EFT reference order and attach the printed transfer confirmation. This is what makes a payment on the ADI statement traceable back to source.

**Payments to an ADI.** Record both: for a trust cheque payable to your bank to obtain a bank cheque for Revenue NSW, the payee is "[Bank] B/C Revenue NSW".

**Cancelled cheques** (error found before issue, original in hand): cross "Cancelled" and tear off the signature; cross the butt or requisition "Cancelled" with the reason; retain and staple the cheque to the butt; enter in the cash book in cheque number sequence with a nil value; do not post to a ledger under a manual system.

**Reversed cheques** (already issued): contact the payee; instruct the ADI to stop payment; rewrite the cash book entry as a **negative** amount with the reason; post to the **credit** side of the ledger; reissue if required. You must hold the original cheque or have stopped it before cancelling.

**Multi cheques.** One cheque to one payee covering several matters is acceptable.

**Stale cheques** (more than 15 months since drawn). Stop with the ADI and reverse in the records using the reversal procedure. A principal should review unpresented cheques on the monthly reconciliation and note the date drawn against each. A cheque unpresented for 3 months is unlikely ever to be presented. *Law Society view:* review everything over three months old and consider cancelling and reissuing rather than waiting for staleness.

### 7.5 Authorised signatories (r 43(2))

Provision: [r 43(2)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.43)

A cheque must be signed by, or an EFT effected under the direction or authority of:

- an **authorised principal**; or
- if no such principal is available: an authorised legal practitioner associate; or an authorised Australian legal practitioner holding a certificate authorising the receipt of trust money; or **two or more authorised associates jointly**.

"Authorised" is not defined. In practice a person is authorised when the practice makes them a signatory or gives them internet banking access.

**A non-practitioner cannot be given sole delegation.** Delegating cheque signing to a long-serving legal secretary alone does not comply; it must be two associates jointly.

**Annual notification.** During July each year, notify the Law Society Council in writing of the associates and Australian legal practitioners (names and addresses) authorised as at 1 July to sign GTA cheques or effect, direct or authorise withdrawals ([r 50(2)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.50)). Not required if already provided in the EER under [s 159](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.159), which is the usual route via Part B. "Associate" includes principals. **No notification is required** for controlled money account signatories.

**Online banking.** The password is the signature on the cheque. Do not entrust passwords to a bookkeeper or office manager. Retain original ADI statements and printed transaction confirmations. Review audit trails so transactions can be traced through the records. Document the process and review it. Where the bookkeeper or office manager effects EFTs, require two persons.

### 7.6 Cash books (rr 44, 45)

Provisions: [rr 44](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.44), [45](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.45)

A receipts cash book and a payments cash book recording all receipts, receipt reversals, receipt cancellations, cheques, cheque reversals and cheque cancellations.

- **Receipts cash book**: all particulars on the trust receipt except the practice name, the "trust account" expression and the maker's name; recorded **in the order the receipts are made out**; plus the date and amount of each deposit.
- **Payments cash book**: the information on the cheque butt, requisition or EFT record; recorded **in the order cheques or EFTs were made or cancelled**.
- **Both within 5 working days** of the receipt or payment.

Format is a matter for the practice provided the required information is captured. Put column headings on every page.

**Posting.** Post individual entries to the trust ledgers daily; post cash book totals to the control account monthly.

| Transaction | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Receipt | Control account (monthly total) | Trust ledger account (daily) |
| Receipt reversal | Trust ledger account | Control account |
| Payment | Trust ledger account (daily) | Control account (monthly total) |
| Payment reversal | Control account | Trust ledger account |
| Transfer journal | Ledger transferred **from** | Ledger transferred **to** |

A journal entry has **no effect on the control account**. Reversals are posted on the opposite side to the original entry.

### 7.7 Trust ledger accounts (r 47)

Provision: [r 47](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.47)

A **separate ledger account for each matter for each person** on whose behalf money is held ([r 47(1)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.47)).

**Ledger heading** must record and keep up to date: the name of the person for and on behalf of whom the money was paid; the person's address; particulars sufficient to identify the matter ([r 47(2)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.47)).

**Transaction particulars** ([r 47(3)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.47)):

1. Date of the transaction
2. Reference number and transaction type
3. Particulars sufficient to identify the reason
4. Amount
5. Plus, by type:
   - **Receipt**: the provider, and the date received if different from the date of receipt
   - **Cheque**: the payee, or for a cheque to an ADI, the ADI name or BSB and the beneficiary
   - **EFT**: the account name and number, the BSB, and the beneficiary
   - **Journal**: the ledger reference, the person on whose behalf the transfer was made, and the matter description

Transactions recorded **in the order they occur**, within **5 working days**, with the **balance shown after each transaction** ([r 47(4)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.47)).

**Ledgers in the practice's own name** are permitted only ([r 49(2)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.49)):

- as a **costs clearing account** aggregating money properly due to the practice for legal costs, which must be withdrawn from the GTA **within one month** of the transfer in ([r 49(3)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.49)); or
- in a legal practitioner associate's name where the associate has a personal and beneficial interest as vendor, purchaser, lessor, lessee or similar, withdrawn **at the conclusion of the matter** ([r 49(4)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.49)).

Aggregating fees only makes sense for very large practices. Otherwise transfer as soon as the costs are drawable.

### 7.8 Trust transfer journal (r 46)

Provision: [r 46](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.46)

Records all non-cash transactions: transfers between matters, correction of incorrect postings, and transfers to a costs clearing account.

**Permitted only if** the practice is entitled to withdraw the money and pay it to the other ledger ([r 46(1)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.46)).

**Must be authorised in writing** by an authorised principal or, if unavailable, an authorised legal practitioner associate, an authorised Australian legal practitioner holding a certificate authorising the receipt of trust money, two authorised associates jointly, or an external intervener. Retain particulars of each authorisation ([r 46(2)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.46)).

**Particulars** ([r 46(3)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.46)): date of transfer; the ledger transferred **from** (ledger reference, person's name, matter description); the ledger transferred **to** (same); particulars identifying the reason; and the amount to and from each ledger. Journal pages or entries must be **consecutively numbered** ([r 46(4)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.46)).

**Journals are a known fraud vector.** Authorise every entry before processing. Check narrations are clear and appropriate to the matter. Scrutinise entries to the control account and any journal used to correct a debit balance. Use a journal transfer request form as the source record, signed by the authorised person, and initial each entry on the printed journal.

## 8. Month end

Two statements, both **as at the end of each named month** and both **within 15 working days** of month end ([r 48](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.48)).

### 8.1 What each statement tests

| Statement | Compares | Failure means |
|---|---|---|
| **Trust trial balance** ([r 48(2)(b)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.48)) | Sum of the individual trust ledgers **against** the reconciled cash book balance | Something has been recorded against the wrong client |
| **ADI reconciliation** ([r 48(2)(a)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.48)) | Reconciled cash book balance **against** the ADI statement | Money may be missing. A deficiency |

### 8.2 Producing the cash book balance

Balance the cash book monthly, either by carrying the balance forward or into a **cash book control account** (the Law Society's preference). Either a "cash book summary" (opening balance plus receipts less payments equals closing balance, recorded in the same place and format each month) or a "cash book balance" (opening balance as the first receipts cash book entry, totalled at month end) is acceptable. If overdrawn, record the balance as the first entry of the payments cash book.

### 8.3 The ADI reconciliation

Procedure:

1. Enter all receipts and payments before month end closure.
2. Total both cash books to produce the closing cash book balance.
3. Obtain the month end ADI statement.
4. Tick items common to both. List separately: items on the ADI statement not in the cash book; and items in the cash book not on the ADI statement (outstanding deposits for receipts; unpresented cheques and other items for payments). Investigate everything except current unpresented cheques.
5. Complete the statement, starting from the ADI closing balance and adding or subtracting the lists, arriving at a **reconciled cash book balance** that agrees with step 2.

**Format:**

- ADI statement balance as at [date] (normally a credit balance)
- Add outstanding deposits
- Add/less items on the ADI statement not in the cash book
- Less unpresented cheques (listed separately with date, cheque number and amount)
- Add/less items in the cash book not on the ADI statement
- = Reconciled cash book balance
- Date of preparation and preparer

The reconciled cash book balance must appear on the statement ([r 48(2)(b)(i)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.48)).

### 8.4 The trial balance

**Required content** ([r 48(2)(b)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.48)): the month it refers to; the date of preparation; for each ledger account the account name, matter reference, short matter description and month end balance; the total of all ledger balances; and a reconciliation of that total to the reconciled cash book balance.

Ensure all transactions and journals are posted and each ledger shows the correct running balance before preparing. Nil balance accounts may be excluded. For computerised systems, print the trial balance **as at the last day of the month and before processing any transactions in the next month**.

**The r 48(2)(b)(i) comparison must be shown on the trial balance**, in one of these forms depending on whether the statutory deposit is included in the ledger total:

```
Total trust ledger accounts        xxx.xx
Add statutory deposit (if excluded) xxx.xx
Reconciled cash book balance       xxx.xx
Variance (must be nil)                Nil
```

### 8.5 Principal review checklist

Reviewing the **reconciliation**:

- ADI statement balance agrees.
- Cash book balance agrees the reconciled cash book balance.
- Outstanding deposits are legitimate and banked on the first working day of the next month.
- Cash content agrees to the receipt.
- Unpresented cheque numbers agree to the cash book.
- Age of unpresented cheques reviewed; stale cheques reversed; anything over six months followed up.
- Adjusting items reversed correctly next month or recorded in the cash book.
- Prior month's items all cleared.
- **Cast the reconciliation** to confirm it actually adds up.

Reviewing the **trial balance**:

- Date of preparation is close to month end.
- **No debit balances.** A debit balance is an overdrawn ledger and an irregularity. Require staff to bring debit balances to your attention *before* correcting them.
- Balances with no movement reviewed. Show the date of last activity. If the matter is complete, the money should be returned.
- The r 48(2)(b)(i) comparison agrees; confirm to the cash book and reconciliation.

### 8.6 ADI errors, unidentified deposits and interest

**ADI errors** (cheque book costs, interest, incorrect deposits) are **not** recorded in the cash book or posted to a ledger. Show them as an adjusting item on the reconciliation and ask the ADI to reverse them directly. Confirm the reversal. On opening the account, ensure the ADI charges account maintenance costs to the **office** account.

**Unidentified deposits.** If not identified by the end of the month following receipt and all enquiries are exhausted, *Law Society view:* record it in the cash book and post it to a ledger account named "Unidentified deposit credited to ADI statement on [date]", or "Client to be identified". If never identified, pay it to unclaimed money under [Application Act s 14](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-016#sec.14).

**Interest on the GTA** is payable to the Law Society for the Public Purpose Fund. It should not be credited to the trust or office account. If the ADI credits it in error, show it as an adjusting item and have the ADI reverse it.

### 8.7 Computerised systems (rr 38-41)

Provision: [rr 38-41](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.38)

**Monthly copies** in printed or readable-and-printable form, unmodifiable after production, as at the end of each named month ([r 38](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.38)):

- ADI reconciliation
- Trust trial balance
- Receipts and payments cash books
- Controlled money listing

Available no later than 15 working days after month end. Also keep (recommended, not required): the transfer journal, the file maintenance audit journal and the ADI statement.

**Backups** ([r 41(2)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.41)): a back-up copy of **all** records required under the Law and Rules **at least monthly**; each copy kept by the practice; and a **complete set kept in a separate location** so that any incident affecting the records would not affect the backup.

**Test the restore function.** A backup you have never restored is not a backup.

**Cloud is not a backup.** Cloud software mirrors what you do. Delete something and the cloud deletes it. The provider will not roll back to a previous version for you. You need your own backup and you need to have tested restoring from it. The same applies when migrating systems: closed matters are routinely left behind.

**Software.** Off-the-shelf general accounting software normally does not comply. The Law Society examines packages on supplier request and issues a Certificate of Examination containing an opinion as to compliance **if competently and properly operated**; certificates may carry qualifications, so read them. Examination is not compulsory and the Law Society does not recommend any package. List of examined software: [Law Society trust accounting software page](https://www.lawsociety.com.au/practising-law-in-NSW/trust-money-and-fidelity-fund/trust-accounting-software).

## 9. Taking your costs out - rule 42

This is the provision that makes a trust account commercially worth having. Follow it exactly: getting it wrong produces both a breach and, usually, a client complaint.

### 9.1 The enabling provision

A practice may withdraw trust money from a GTA or CMA for payment to its own account for legal costs owing, **if the procedures in the Uniform Rules are complied with** ([s 144(2)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.144)). The procedures are in [r 42](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.42), which prescribes four methods.

Rule 42 applies to the **GTA and CMA only**. It does **not** apply to power money or investment of trust money, where costs may be taken only under the power or on the person's instructions.

**Legal costs** means amounts charged or which may become payable to the practice for legal services, including disbursements but **not** interest ([s 6](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.6)).

### 9.2 The four methods

| Method | Rule | Requirement | Wait |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. No authority** | [r 42(3)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.42) | Bill given **referring to the proposed withdrawal** | **7 business days** with no objection; or **30 days** after the later of the bill date and receipt of an itemised bill where the person objects but has not referred the matter to the Commissioner or for assessment; or the money otherwise becomes legally payable |
| **2. Instructions received** | [r 42(4)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.42) | Instructions authorising the withdrawal, **and** before withdrawing, a request for payment referring to the proposed withdrawal **or** a written notice of withdrawal | None |
| **3. Reimbursement** | [r 42(5)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.42) | Money owed by way of reimbursement of a disbursement **already paid**, **and** a request for payment or written notice of withdrawal sent first | None |
| **4. Commercial or government client** | [r 42(6)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.42) | Bill given specifying the amount; withdrawal in accordance with a compliant costs agreement that **authorises the withdrawal**; and a request for payment referring to the proposed withdrawal sent first | None |

### 9.3 Method 1 in detail

**"Referring to the proposed withdrawal" is a separate requirement** and the one most often missed. *Law Society view:* include a footer on the bill to the effect that "It is intended to withdraw the above amount from money held in your trust ledger at the expiration of 7 business days from the date of this bill unless an objection is received."

**The bill must also comply with the billing provisions:**

- Signed by a principal designated as the **responsible principal** for the bill, or nominating one. If neither, **every principal is taken to be responsible** ([s 188](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.188)).
- Given in accordance with [s 189](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.189) and [r 73](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.73).
- Including or accompanied by a **written statement** of the avenues open to the client in a costs dispute and any applicable time limits ([s 192](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.192)).

### 9.4 Method 2 in detail

Instructions in writing must be kept as a permanent record. Oral instructions must be **confirmed in writing before, or not later than 5 working days after, the withdrawal**, with a copy kept ([r 42(7)(b)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.42)). *Law Society view:* the practice must initiate the confirming correspondence and keep a copy on the matter file.

**A blanket authorisation is not sufficient** because it does not refer to a definite sum. Where the authorisation covers part only, you may withdraw to that extent only; if you have also given a bill under Method 1, r 42(4)(b) applies to the remainder ([r 42(7)(a)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.42)).

**Do not rely on oral authority plus later confirmation.** Get the written authority up front. A thumbs-up by email in response to your notice of intended withdrawal is sufficient if it clearly came from the client. Messaging apps are problematic because provenance is hard to evidence.

### 9.5 Method 3 in detail

Money is taken to have been paid on the person's behalf **when the practice's account has been debited** ([r 42(8)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.42)). *Law Society view:* the cheque paying the disbursement must be debited to the office account.

You cannot transfer from trust and then pay. Pay first, wait for the debit, then reimburse. This catches practices that pay disbursements by credit card.

### 9.6 Method 4 in detail

**Commercial or government client** is defined at [s 170(2)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.170): law practices; specified *Corporations Act* entities; certain joint venture groups; large professional partnerships; bodies incorporated outside Australia; persons who agreed costs through a tender; government authorities; and persons specified in the Uniform Rules.

### 9.7 The consequences of getting it wrong

A transfer without authority is a failure to hold funds in accordance with [s 138(1)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.138) or [s 139(2)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.139), capable of being unsatisfactory professional conduct or professional misconduct ([s 298](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.298)). Ensure the authority covers both the transfer and the amount.

Examiners look for trust-to-office transfers in the cash book and then ask for the bill to check the timing.

### 9.8 Draw promptly once you can

Money for services provided and billed is no longer trust money ([s 129(2)(a)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.129)). Leaving it in trust is intermixing under [s 146](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.146) and costs you cash flow.

### 9.9 The costs framework Methods 1 and 4 depend on

- **Disclosure** ([ss 174-178](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.174)). Failure means the client need not pay unless costs are assessed or the dispute determined ([s 178(1)(c)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.178)); the costs agreement is **void**; the practice **cannot sue**; and the failure may be unsatisfactory professional conduct or professional misconduct ([s 178(1)(a), (d)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.178)). No disclosure required below **$750** excluding GST and disbursements ([s 174(4)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.174)).
- **Costs agreements** ([ss 179-185](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.179)). Must be in writing or evidenced in writing. **Conditional costs agreements** must be in plain language, set out the successful outcome, be signed by the client, state that the client was informed of the right to independent advice, and contain a cooling-off period of not less than **five clear business days** ([s 181](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.181)).
- **Contingency fees are prohibited** ([s 183](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.183)).
- A non-compliant costs agreement is **void**; on a s 183 contravention the practice recovers **nothing** and must repay ([s 185](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.185)).

## 10. Controlled money

### 10.1 Setting it up

**A written direction is the gateway.** Obtain it before depositing ([s 139(1)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.139)). *Law Society view:* the direction should record the matter, the reason for the investment, the ADI and the account type, and where money is held for two or more parties, **how interest is to be distributed**. Do not advise the client on which ADI or product to choose: the practice risks breaching the *Corporations Act 2001* (Cth) unless it holds an AFSL.

**Retain the direction for 7 years** ([s 139(5)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.139)), in the controlled money register with a copy on the matter file.

**Account name must include** ([r 61](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.61)): the practice name; the expression "controlled money account", "CMA" or "CMA/c"; and particulars sufficient to identify the purpose and distinguish it from other accounts. So: practice name, CMA, client and matter identifier.

**No pooling** ([s 139(6)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.139)). Held exclusively for the person directing.

Practical points: include "ITF" in the account name to remove the trustee return requirement; give the **client's** tax file number to the ADI, never the practice's, so that interest is not recorded as credited to the practice and withholding does not apply.

**No written direction?** A cheque payable to the practice, or cash, goes to the GTA pending written instructions. A cheque payable to the client is transit money and must be delivered to the client or returned to the drawer.

### 10.2 Controlled money receipts (r 62)

Provision: [r 62](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.62)

Made out **as soon as practicable** after receipt; for direct deposits, after notice or confirmation from the ADI. Given to the payer on request.

**One receipt system for all controlled money accounts**, including across branches.

**Form**: duplicate (unless the particulars are recorded by a computerised system in the controlled money register at the time); practice name or business name; the expression "controlled money receipt"; consecutively numbered; issued in sequence.

**Required particulars:**

1. Date the receipt is made out
2. Date of receipt of the money, if different
3. Receipt number
4. Form in which received
5. Name of the person from whom received
6. Name of the person on whose behalf received, matter description and matter reference
7. Name and details clearly identifying the CMA to be credited, **unless the account is not yet established**
8. Particulars identifying the reason for the receipt
9. Name of the person who made out the receipt
10. Amount received

If the account was not established when the receipt issued, record the account particulars in the **duplicate**. No receipt is required for interest credited directly to the CMA. Where money already in the GTA is later directed into a CMA, a controlled money receipt is made out **to the general trust account**.

### 10.3 Payments out (r 63)

Provision: [r 63](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.63)

**Only** in accordance with the original written direction or a later written direction from the person, or under rule 42 for legal costs, or under a court order or as authorised by law.

**Only by cheque or EFT.** Prohibited: cash withdrawals; ATM withdrawals or transfers; telephone banking; **BPAY**.

**Who may authorise** ([r 63(2)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.63)), sequentially: an authorised principal; if unavailable, an authorised legal practitioner associate; if that person is unavailable, an authorised Australian legal practitioner holding a certificate authorising the receipt of trust money; if both are unavailable, two or more associates jointly.

**Written record of each withdrawal**, filed in payment order and kept **separately for each CMA**:

1. Date of the transaction
2. Transaction number
3. For EFT, the name and account number including BSB transferred to; for cheque, the payee; for a cheque to an ADI, the ADI name or BSB and the beneficiary
4. Name of the person on whose behalf the payment was made and the matter reference
5. Particulars sufficient to identify the purpose
6. Amount withdrawn
7. The person effecting, directing or authorising the withdrawal

### 10.4 Controlled money register and movement records (r 64)

Provision: [r 64](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.64)

A register holding, for all CMAs: written directions; cheque and payment records; the movement record for each account; the monthly listing; ADI statements and interest notifications; account statements; and supporting information.

**Movement record heading**: the person's name and address; particulars sufficient to identify the matter; and any changes to that information.

**On receipt**, record: the date received; receipt number; date deposited; name and details of the CMA; amount deposited; details sufficient to identify the deposit; interest received.

**On withdrawal**, record: the date and number of the payment; the destination account (EFT) or payee (cheque) or ADI and beneficiary; the person on whose behalf paid and the matter reference; the purpose; the authorised person effecting the withdrawal; and the amount.

**Timing**: as soon as practicable after receipt or payment. Interest and other income: as soon as practicable after the practice is notified.

### 10.5 Monthly controlled money listing (r 64(8)-(9))

Provision: [r 64(8)-(9)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.64)

As at the end of each month, in permanent form, **within 15 working days**, showing: the month; the date of preparation; and for each account, the name, number and balance, the person on whose behalf held, and a short description of the matter.

**Must be reviewed by a principal who is authorised to receive trust money, and the review evidenced on the statement** ([r 64(9)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.64)).

## 11. Records for the other categories

### 11.1 Transit money

Record and keep **brief particulars sufficient to identify the transaction and any purpose** for which the money was received ([s 140(2)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.140)), for **7 years** ([s 140(3)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.140)).

In practice: copies of third-party cheques, settlement directions, directions from an incoming mortgagee or to an agent, kept on the client file. No cash book, ledger, journal or trust account statement.

### 11.2 Written direction money

Keep the written direction as part of the trust records for **7 years after finalisation of the matter** ([s 142](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.142); [s 147(2)(c)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.147)). Keep originals in a single folder with copies on the matter files, plus a copy of the cheque. No cash book, ledger, journal or trust account statement.

Where a client directs that a cheque payable to the practice's trust account be endorsed over to them, this is permissible if the ADI accepts the endorsement, **provided** the direction is in writing, a receipt on the client's letterhead is obtained, and the direction and a copy of the endorsed cheque are kept as trust records.

### 11.3 Power money (r 55)

Provision: [r 55](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.55)

Keep a **record of all dealings** with the money to which the practice or associate is a party, and **all supporting information**, in a manner that enables the dealings to be clearly understood. *Law Society view:* keep it in ledger form.

Retain the record, the supporting information and the power itself for **7 years** ([s 147(2)(c)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.147)), including bank statements, payee details, supporting invoices and receipts, and cheque books.

Where two practices are joint signatories on one account with a single bank statement, they may agree that one keeps the records provided the other receives copies at least monthly.

**Register of Powers and Estates** ([r 60](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.60)). Must record:

- particulars sufficient to identify each **power** the practice or an associate is acting or entitled to act under, including the **donor's name and address and the date** of the power;
- particulars sufficient to identify each **estate**, including the **name and date of death of the deceased** where the practice or associate is executor or administrator.

**Not required** where the practice or associate must act jointly with someone who is **not** an associate ([r 60(2)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.60)).

Scope is wide: a sole general power of attorney given to a legal practitioner associate must be entered **regardless of whether** the practitioner becomes a signatory on the client's account.

### 11.4 Investment of trust money (r 59)

Provision: [r 59](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.59)

**Register of investments** recording, for each investment:

1. The name in which the investment is held
2. The name of the person on whose behalf it is made
3. That person's address
4. Particulars sufficient to identify the investment
5. Amount invested
6. Date invested
7. Particulars sufficient to identify the **source** of the investment
8. Details of documents evidencing the investment
9. Details of interest received or credited directly
10. Details of repayment of the investment and any interest

*Law Society view* on item 7: include a reference to the relevant trust ledger, a reference to the written authority to invest, and the cheque number for the amount invested.

Not required if the particulars are recorded elsewhere, for example in the controlled money register. Attach a copy of the document evidencing the investment (for example a share certificate).

## 12. Trust account statements

Governed by [rr 52](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.52) and [53](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.53).

**Who gets one.** Every person for whom or on whose behalf trust money **other than transit money and written direction money** is held or controlled ([r 52(1)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.52)). That means general trust money, controlled money, power money and investment of trust money.

**One statement per record** ([r 52(2)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.52)): per trust ledger account; per controlled money movement record; per record of dealings with power money.

**Content** ([r 52(3)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.52)): all information required to be kept in the relevant ledger account or record, plus the remaining balance. In substance it mirrors the ledger. Retain a copy.

**When** ([r 52(4)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.52)), as soon as practicable after:

- completion of the matter;
- a reasonable request by the person during the matter;
- **30 June** each year.

*Law Society view on timing:* since the ledger is not validated until the reconciliation and trial balance are done (15 working days after month end), the 30 June statements need not reasonably be given until mid to late July.

**Exemption from the 30 June statement** ([r 52(5)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.52)) where the balance is **zero** at 30 June **and** either no transaction has occurred in the previous 12 months, or a statement was furnished in the previous 12 months and no transaction has occurred since.

**Commercial and government clients** may direct that no statement be given, or that statements be given on a different basis, which the practice must follow unless unreasonably onerous ([r 53](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.53)).

### 12.1 The person is not always your client

The obligation runs to **the person on whose behalf the money is held**. In a conveyance, a deposit is held for both vendor and purchaser. In a family law matter, sale proceeds before settlement are held for both parties. Consequences:

- Statements go to **every person with an interest**, not only your client.
- **Costs cannot be taken from joint money without the authorisation of both parties.**
- **Disbursement requires the approval of each party** with an interest.

The statement also functions as a control: a client who receives a statement is likely to query anything wrong on it.

## 13. Statutory deposit

### 13.1 The obligation

Out of money paid to the GTA, the practice must deposit with the Law Society and keep deposited an amount not less than the calculated minimum ([Application Act s 46](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-016#sec.46); [Application Regulation s 11](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2025-0453#sec.11)). Repayable on demand ([Application Act s 47](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-016#sec.47)). Interest goes to the Public Purpose Fund ([s 47(4)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-016#sec.47)).

**Each principal must take reasonable steps to ensure compliance** ([Application Regulation s 11(2)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2025-0453#sec.11)).

**Numbering note.** The 2015 Regulation was repealed on 1 September 2025 and remade as the 2025 Regulation. The statutory deposit provisions are now [ss 10-15](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2025-0453#sec.10): s 10 definitions, s 11 obligation, s 12 minimum amount, s 13 timing, s 14 inability to comply, s 15 Law Society records. Verify the current text before relying on any calculation step.

### 13.2 The calculation

- **Applicable period**: the 3 months ending 31 March, 30 June, 30 September or 31 December ([Application Regulation s 10](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2025-0453#sec.10)).
- Calculated on the **ADI statement balance**, not the reconciled cash book balance, plus any amount already on statutory deposit.
- **Threshold: if the combined balance is under $10,000, no deposit is required.**

**Base amount** = the lowest balance in the trust account during the **previous applicable period**, plus the amount on statutory deposit on the day that lowest balance was recorded.

**The 80% look-forward.** If, on any day from the end of the previous applicable period to the **15th banking day** after it, the equivalent sum is **lower** than the base amount, the minimum becomes **80% of that lower sum**.

**After a repayment out of the statutory deposit during a period**, recalculate on the lower of the relevant minimum balances, measured to the 15th banking day after the repayment. Apply to each repayment separately.

Round up to the next hundred dollars.

### 13.3 Timing

Deposit into a statutory deposit account **held by the same authorised ADI that maintains the GTA**, not later than **20 banking days** after the end of the applicable period, or after a repayment ([Application Regulation s 13](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2025-0453#sec.13)).

**If you cannot**, request a determination from the Law Society ([Application Regulation s 14](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2025-0453#sec.14)) using the [Request for Determination form](https://www.lawsociety.com.au/practising-law-in-NSW/trust-money-and-fidelity-fund/statutory-deposits). Once determined, deposit within **5 banking days** of notification. No recalculation is then required for that period.

Circumstances requiring a determination: the deposit would overdraw the ADI statement balance or the cash book, or leave insufficient funds to operate the GTA.

### 13.4 Practical mechanics

- Open a **Statutory Deposit ledger account**. Deposits are debits; withdrawals are credits.
- Where a statutory deposit is already held, remember the GTA balance has **two elements at all times**: the ADI statement balance and the statutory deposit on the same day. Include both when finding the minimum balance.
- **Withdrawals**: attend the ADI with the letter of credit and request a transfer to the GTA. You determine the amount; no Law Society permission is needed. Money withdrawn must go **directly into the GTA** and a **trust receipt must be issued**.
- **Withdrawing from the GTA to fund the deposit**: record the payment in the trust cash book.
- **Multiple accounts**: separate calculations and separate deposits for a head office and a branch with separate ledgers and accounts, or for one office with two ledgers and two accounts. Aggregate only where one office has **one** ledger and two ADI accounts.
- **No notification** to the Law Society of deposits or withdrawals is needed; it receives monthly ADI statements. No trust account statement is sent to the Law Society.
- Use the [Statutory Deposit Calculator](https://www.lawsociety.com.au/practising-law-in-NSW/trust-money-and-fidelity-fund/statutory-deposits/calculator). Difficulty: Trust Accounts Department on (02) 9926 0337.

**What examiners check**: that you have performed the calculation. They do not do it for you. A missing statutory deposit is one of the most frequently recorded breaches.

**Opening the account**: write to the ADI on letterhead, signed by the signatories, requesting a statutory deposit account. If branch staff cannot assist, email accounts@lawsociety.com.au identifying which ADI holds your GTA. Confirm you have actually been given a statutory deposit account and not a second general trust account.

## 14. Irregularities

### 14.1 What counts

Not defined in the legislation, deliberately. The working test: **the trust account is not behaving as it should**. Examples:

- Any error or deficiency in a trust account or trust ledger, including any **debit balance**
- Intermixing trust money with other money
- Unauthorised withdrawals, or failure to obtain proper authorisation
- Fraud, cyber-crime, or receipt of trust money from unknown or unexpected sources
- Any other irregularity in the receipt, recording or disbursement of trust money

The commonest in practice: money accidentally transferred from trust to office; an office expense accidentally paid from trust; an overdrawn ledger; an overdrawn trust account.

### 14.2 Who must report (s 154)

Provision: [s 154](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.154)

**Section 154(1)**: written notice to the DLRA when an associate of the practice, an Australian legal practitioner in the practice, an external examiner, an ADI, or another specified entity **becomes aware** of an irregularity.

**Section 154(2)**: an Australian legal practitioner **external** to the practice must notify **as soon as practicable** after forming a belief on reasonable grounds that there is an irregularity in another practice's trust money. This is effectively a whistleblowing duty. Continued non-response to enquiries about your client's money held by another practice can found the belief.

**You cannot decline** ([s 466](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.466)). Compliance is required despite legal professional privilege, any other duty of confidence, or the risk of self-incrimination. Failure is capable of being unsatisfactory professional conduct or professional misconduct, and the DLRA may **suspend your practising certificate** while the failure continues.

Report via the Law Society's [irregularity reporting process](https://www.lawsociety.com.au/practising-law-nsw/trust-money-and-fidelity-fund/trust-account-irregularity-reporting/s154), addressed to the Chief Trust Account Investigator, stating the reasons and the evidence of rectification.

### 14.3 What to do

1. **Stop.** Where there is a deficiency, suspend all activity on the trust account until it is resolved or made good. You cannot operate a trust account while a deficiency exists.
2. **Make it good.** You may contribute your own funds. That is what lets you keep operating.
3. **Report** to the DLRA.
4. **Take advice** from the DLRA.
5. **Recover** from the client afterwards if appropriate. Once you have replaced the money it is no longer trust money.

**Report it even if you have already fixed it.** If the examiner finds it and you did not report, the examiner reports the failure to report.

### 14.4 Who bears the loss

| Cause | Outcome |
|---|---|
| Fraud by the practitioner | Fidelity fund engaged |
| Negligence | A LawCover claim is doubtful |
| Error | Pro rata distribution of available trust money if the practitioner cannot make good |

And the practice cannot continue to operate the trust account while the deficiency exists.

### 14.5 Why deficiencies surface late

The practice does not notify under s 154; the EER is not due until 31 May, so an April defalcation may not surface for thirteen months; and detection often comes only from follow-up of outstanding declarations. **Which is why the monthly reconciliation matters.** If you fall behind, stop transacting on the account.

## 15. Annual compliance and external examination

### 15.1 The cycle

The trust year is **1 April to 31 March** ([s 155(1)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.155)).

| What | Deadline |
|---|---|
| **Part A - Confirmation of Trust Money**, required of **every** law practice whether or not trust money was held ([r 54](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.54)) | **30 April** |
| **Part B - Statement of Trust Money**, if applicable | **31 May** |
| **External Examiner's Report**, if applicable | **31 May** |

Lodged through the Trust Lodgement Portal via the Law ID portal. The Law Society emails all practices on 31 March.

**Part A** (about two minutes) confirms your practice details, area-of-practice percentages, and which types of trust money were held or received. A Part B and EER are required if you held **general trust money, controlled money, trust money subject to a specific power, or investment trust money**.

**Part B** (about fifteen minutes) requires the March end-of-month records, collation of the year's records, reporting of all irregularities, updating of authorised signatories (which also discharges [r 50(2)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.50)), and upload of the 31 March bank reconciliation.

**Do Part B when your March reconciliation is done**, not on 31 May. Your deadline and the examiner's are the same date, and the examiner has to examine Part B.

### 15.2 When an examination is required

- **Annually**, where the practice received or held trust money ([s 155(1)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.155)).
- **On cessation**, where the practice ceases to be authorised to receive trust money or ceases legal practice and has held a trust account ([r 68](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.68)). The final report is lodged **60 days** from the end of the period examined.

**Not required** if the practice only received or held: power money under an electronic lodgement network operator's settlement scheme (such as PEXA); written direction money; and/or transit money.

**No de minimis.** $10 of general trust money triggers the requirement. A dispensation can be sought, and dispensations are granted; failing that, negotiate a fixed fee.

### 15.3 Who may examine (r 65, s 156)

Provisions: [r 65](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.65), [s 156](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.156)

Members of CPA Australia, Chartered Accountants Australia and New Zealand, or the Institute of Public Accountants holding a current public practice certificate; persons registered as auditors under Part 9.2 of the *Corporations Act 2001* (Cth); or employees or agents of the DLRA. Classes other than DLRA staff must also have completed an approved external examiner course run by the Law Society of NSW, the Law Institute of Victoria or the Legal Practice Board of WA.

**An associate of the practice cannot examine that practice.** Find one via the [External Examiners search portal](https://az-ae-app-external-examiners-search-webportal-prd.azurewebsites.net/ExternalExaminersSearch/Search). The DLRA will not recommend anyone.

**Appoint early.** Examiners plan their season and will decline new work once allocated.

**Termination requires prior Law Society Council approval** ([r 66(3)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.66)).

### 15.4 Examiner powers and the report

An examiner may require the practice, or any person who has or had control of documents relating to its affairs, to give **access to documents** and **information** (verified by statutory declaration if required) ([s 370](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.370)), and may inspect, copy or retain them.

The report goes to the Law Society Council in the standard form ([s 159](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.159); [r 67](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.67)) and must cover **all** GTAs and accounts holding trust money opened or closed during the period.

### 15.5 What the examination covers

Cash book entries (looking for large amounts, anything unusual, and trust-to-office transfers, which then determine which ledgers get examined); month end procedures; the registers; supporting documentation; statutory deposits; irregularity notifications; and rule 42 compliance. The Law Society publishes an [External Examiner Checklist](https://lawsociety.com.au/sites/default/files/2025-04/External%20Examiners%20Checklist%202025.pdf).

Examinations are usually remote, by production of reports and documents. If you grant software access, create a **read-only login for the examiner**, never share your own credentials.

### 15.6 Keeping examiner fees down

- Ask for a **fixed fee**, particularly in the early years of a small trust account.
- Keep compliant records and use compliant software. Messy records take longer.
- Lodge Part A and Part B early.
- **Answer queries concisely and completely.** Every unanswered question means the examiner reopens the file and bills for it.
- Do not send padded or AI-generated responses to a draft report. The examiner must read it and bill for reading it. If the examiner says there is a breach, the useful answer is short and evidentiary: "We do obtain written instructions, and here they are."

## 16. Investigations

The Law Society Council may investigate, or appoint an external investigator to investigate, the affairs or specified affairs of a law practice ([s 162](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.162)).

**Purpose**: to ascertain compliance with Part 4.2 and to detect and prevent fraud or defalcation, without limiting the scope of the investigation ([s 161](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.161)).

**Two triggers** ([s 163](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.163)): particular allegations or suspicions; or where the Council otherwise considers it appropriate. The second supports a programme of **routine** investigations scheduled by perceived risk. Allegation-driven investigations take priority.

**Powers**: access to documents and information relating to the practice's affairs, verified by statutory declaration if required ([s 370](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.370)). For incorporated legal practices, additional powers to examine persons, inspect books and hold meetings ([ss 383](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.383)-[385](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.385)).

**"Affairs"** includes all accounts and records required to be maintained, other records, and any transaction to which the practice or an associate or former associate was a party or in which it acted for a party ([s 6](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.6)).

**Your obligations**: comply with requirements for access and information ([s 370(2)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.370)); do not obstruct an investigator without reasonable excuse ([s 387](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.387)); do not mislead an investigator ([s 388](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.388)). Privilege, confidence, a lien over documents and self-incrimination are **not excuses** ([s 466](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.466)).

**Outcomes**: investigation letters are issued for breaches considered not wilful, copied to the external examiner, and discussed with the principal. **Wilful or substantial** breaches produce a report under [s 165](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.165) addressing each element of the offence, which may go to Professional Standards. Conduct matters must be referred to the Council ([s 386](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.386)).

**Costs**: where the DLRA is satisfied a contravention or default is **wilful or of a substantial nature**, it may recover all or part of the reasonable costs of the investigation as a debt from the practice ([s 166](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.166)).

## 17. The three practice registers

These apply to **every** law practice, trust money or not.

### 17.1 Opening files (r 91E)

Provision: [r 91E](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.91E)

A file must be opened for **each matter** for which the practice receives instructions, **as soon as practicable** after the instructions, containing or endorsed with the particulars required by [r 93(2)(a)-(d)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.93) and the contact details used to contact the person.

### 17.2 Register of files opened (r 93)

Provision: [r 93](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.93)

For each matter:

- (a) full name and address of the person
- (b) date of receipt of the instructions
- (c) short description of the services agreed to be provided
- (d) an identifier
- (e) **the location of any regulated property relating to the matter**

Where regulated property is a document on a server accessible from multiple locations, record the location as the practice's **principal place of practice** ([r 93(2A)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.93)).

**Regulated property** ([s 6](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.6)): trust money or trust property; anything derived from or acquired with it; documents or records relating to either; any hardware, software or device by which those records may be reproduced in visible form; and **client files**.

Field (e) was introduced in December 2019 and is commonly missing from older systems and manual registers.

### 17.3 Register of safe custody documents (r 94)

Provision: [r 94](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.94)

For each will, deed, document or other valuable property held in safe custody:

- (a) full name and address of the person who gave the instructions
- (b) short description of the item
- (c) date of receipt by the practice
- (d) identifier of the safe custody packet

Keep the register secure. Obtain a **signature for receipt** when documents are released.

### 17.4 Register of financial interests (r 95)

Provision: [r 95](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.95)

For **each legal practitioner associate**:

- (a) full name and address of the associate
- (b) name and identifying particulars of any company, partnership or other entity in which the associate has a financial interest **and which engages in any dealing with trust money** received by the associate or the practice, other than ASX-listed companies and shelf companies maintained for sale

The associate must cause those details to be disclosed **as soon as practicable** ([r 95(3)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.95)).

### 17.5 Requirements common to all three (r 95A)

Provision: [r 95A](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.95A)

Each register must be in **English**; **legible**; kept in a **single document** or in a manner allowing one to be compiled; kept **at the practice premises at all times**; **accessible at all times** to an external intervener or an investigator carrying out a function under [s 368](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-16a#sec.368); and kept up to date.

Entries must be made as soon as practicable after the file is opened, or after the information becomes known.

## 18. Old balances, disputed money and unclaimed money

### 18.1 File closure discipline

Do not close a file until all balances are cleared and there are no unpresented cheques relating to the matter on the trust or office reconciliation. Old balances are both a compliance problem and a theft risk.

### 18.2 Money in dispute

Where the practice holds money as trustee, **is satisfied the money is disputed** and **reasonable efforts have been made to resolve it**, the money may be paid into court under [*Trustee Act 1925* (NSW) s 95](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-1925-014#sec.95). See also Part 55 of the [Uniform Civil Procedure Rules 2005](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2005-0418).

### 18.3 End clauses - prevent the deadlock

Before taking money into trust on a conditional basis, ask: **on whose behalf are we holding it**, and **what conditions release it**.

A clause requiring **both parties to agree** before release may mean the money is never released. Nominate an **independent trigger** instead. Where money is held pending a repair before settlement, release on the property manager's certification that the work is adequately completed, not on agreement between the parties.

### 18.4 Unclaimed money (Application Act s 14)

Provision: [Application Act s 14](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2014-016#sec.14)

Where the practice cannot find the person on whose behalf money is held, or a person authorised to receive it, the practice **may** pay it to the Treasurer for credit to the Consolidated Fund and provide the required information. On doing so the practice is **relieved from further liability**. The Treasurer pays anyone who establishes entitlement.

It is permissive, not mandatory. But review old balances and clear them: holding money you cannot return serves nobody and carries a theft risk. Make reasonable attempts to contact the owner first. Lodge via [Revenue NSW unclaimed money](https://www.revenue.nsw.gov.au/unclaimed-money/lodge).

### 18.5 Refunding unused money

Nothing beyond the ordinary book entries and the **trust account statement** at the close of the matter ([r 52(4)(a)](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2015-0246#sec.52)), on which the final entry records the repayment.

## 19. Controls

### 19.1 Four areas to control

Compliance with the legislation; employed accounting staff; employed solicitors; and partners.

### 19.2 Month end management reports

Call for and review these monthly, within 15 working days: **cash book / control account; trial balance; reconciliation statement; the r 48(2)(b)(i) comparison.** Review by exception. If you cannot review them yourself, engage external staff to review them and report variances to you.

### 19.3 Standing controls

**Deposits and payments:**

- Bank regularly, promptly and **intact as received**.
- Delays in depositing are reported to the principal.
- **Ledger cards sighted before** signing a cheque, CMA withdrawal or EFT authorisation.
- Source records marked to indicate payment.
- Deposits checked against the form of funds shown on the receipts.
- **Cheques, CMA withdrawals and EFT authorisations are never pre-signed.**
- **Trust cheques are never altered by employees.**
- **Personal or office cheques are never cashed from trust funds.**

**Stationery:**

- Retain cancelled cheques and receipts.
- Maintain sequence control over issued cheques and receipts.
- **Lock away unissued cheque books and receipt books.** Otherwise cheques can be taken from the back of an unused book and you will not detect it, because your issued cheques are all in sequence.
- Cover home office and branch offices.

**ADI statements:** receive them regularly and review for dishonoured cheques, a declining GTA balance (which may require a statutory deposit withdrawal), and delays in depositing compared with presented cheques.

**Exception reporting:** accounting staff and employed practitioners must bring anything unusual to the principal's immediate attention.

**Staff:** rotate duties where possible, especially for staff depositing, recording and handling trust money. **Encourage holidays.**

### 19.4 The partner's self-test

1. Does the practice use interim receipt books? If so, are all issued receipts entered into the system, all unissued receipts accounted for, and all branch receipts captured?
2. Does the practice ever issue manual cheques? What ensures the payee on the ledger matches the cheque?
3. Could an employed solicitor be running matters I am unaware of? Do I open all mail into my section, sign all their letters, require a file number on every letter, and know who opens files and records them in the register?
4. Before transferring trust money: has a bill been rendered, and has the client authorised it?
5. Is there an open file policy? **Practices investigated for major conduct problems almost always involve partners keeping problem files under lock and key.**
6. How do I know all funds received are recorded? Is there a file review policy? Are trust ledgers checked against files when signing cheques?

### 19.5 The danger areas

Where misappropriation recurs, and therefore where extra control is warranted:

| Pattern | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Deceased estates** | Long-term investments, no active executor or beneficiary, beneficiaries who may not know they are beneficiaries |
| **Interim receipts** | No control ensuring every interim receipt is processed into the system, so a receipt can be issued without the funds being accounted for |
| **Intermingling with practice funds** | Trust money deposited to the office account |
| **Money into an associated entity** | Trust money deposited to a company associated with the practice, never touching either set of records |
| **Acting for both parties** | Enables false valuations and diversion of duty money |
| **Dishonoured office cheques** | Large office cheques given to clients to placate them temporarily |
| **Powers of attorney** | Wide powers over a client's affairs with nobody checking |
| **False ledger entries** | Payee in the trust record differing from the ledger; false payer details. Watch for **frequent bank cheque purchases where a trust cheque would do** |
| **One client dominating the practice** | Practitioner's and client's affairs become intermingled |
| **Delay or failure in billing** | Bills suppressed, or a trust-to-office transfer made with the cheque never banked and the debtor later written off |
| **Conversion of cheques** | A cheque payable to the client deposited to the solicitor's account or endorsed to a third party |

### 19.6 Risk analysis

Define the practice's principal activities; identify the functions that deliver them; and for each function identify the **risk**, the **cause**, the **effect** and the **prevention and detection measures**. Then decide whether the function is worth controlling, chart it and review it.

Worked example, making a payment. Risk: paying the wrong person and overdrawing the trust account. Worth mitigating: yes. Mitigation: records up to date; ledger balance reviewed before payment; account details checked against the bank's confirmation of account name; record marked as paid after payment.

**Manual records carry an extra risk.** Computer users must back up under rr 38 and 41. There is no equivalent requirement for manual records, and reconstruction after loss, theft or destruction is slow. Keep photocopies of trial balances and the cash book off the premises in a secure location.

### 19.7 Payment fraud and cyber

The dominant risk is **payment redirection**: a compromised email and altered account details.

- **Independently verify account details before paying.** Banks now display the payee account name before you confirm; use it.
- Set a threshold above which you telephone the payee on a number you already hold, not one from the email.
- Require **secondary verification** of payment instructions.
- Use **dual signatories**; require two persons where a bookkeeper or office manager effects EFTs.
- **Review bank statements daily.**
- Two-factor authentication everywhere. Never share passwords; the password is the signature on the cheque.
- Remove system access from departing employees; restrict access to what the role needs; avoid giving staff administrator access.
- Back up, store off site, and **test the restore**.
- Apply exception thinking at the point of payment: do you know the client, is there a genuine open file, and have the payment directions changed late?

Resources: [ACSC Cyber Health Check](https://www.cyber.gov.au/cyberhealthcheck); [ACSC Small Business Cyber Security Guide](https://www.cyber.gov.au/business-government/small-business-cyber-security/small-business-hub/small-business-cyber-security-guide); [Law Society cyber security resources](https://www.lawsociety.com.au/legal-technology/cyber-security-resources). Fuller treatment in the **Cyber Security** guide.

## 20. The breaches examiners actually find

From a recent year's examination data, in descending order:

| Count | Rule | Breach |
|---|---|---|
| 317 | r 48(3) | Reconciliation statements not prepared within 15 days after month end |
| 218 | r 48(3) | Trust trial balances not prepared within 15 days of month end |
| 175 | Application Reg | Statutory deposit review not completed within the required banking days |
| 129 | r 44(3) | Receipts not recorded in the receipts cash book within 5 working days |
| 107 | r 45(2)(b) | Payments not recorded in the payments cash book within 5 days |
| 89 | r 48 | Stale cheques not reversed |
| 74 | r 64(8) | Controlled money listings not prepared within 15 working days of month end |
| 70 | Application Reg | Amount on statutory deposit not in accordance with the practice's own calculation |
| 69 | r 52 | Trust account statements not sent at 30 June |
| 62 | r 47(3)(e)(i) | Trust ledger does not record the form in which money was received |
| 55 | r 61(b) | CMA name lacks "controlled money account", "CMA" or "CMA/c" |
| 52 | r 47(4)(b) | Trust ledger transaction not posted within 5 working days |
| 48 | r 52(4)(a) | Statements not issued as soon as practicable after completion of a matter |
| 47 | r 48(1) | Reconciliation statements not prepared every month |
| 45 | r 48(2)(a)(i) | Reconciliation does not reconcile the ADI balance to the cash book balance |
| 44 | r 43(1)(a) | Cheques not payable to a specified person, or drawn to bearer or cash |

**The pattern**: almost every breach is a **timing** breach on month end procedures or posting. They are entirely avoidable.

Other recurring concerns: debit balances caused by unposted transactions; delays correcting debit balances; late deposits; drawing against uncleared funds; unreceipted direct deposits; old balances; unidentified deposits; insufficient information in source documents, cash books, journals and ledgers; use of suspense accounts; costs clearing accounts not cleared within the month; untested backups; and non-compliant software.

### 22.1 Records by money type

| | GTA money | Controlled money | Transit | Written direction | Power money | Investment |

| Journal | Yes (r 46) | No | No | No | No | No |
| Register | - | Controlled money (r 64) | - | - | Powers and estates (r 60) | Investments (r 59) |
| Monthly reconciliation and trial balance | Yes (r 48) | No | No | No | No | No |
| Monthly listing | No | Yes (r 64(8)) | No | No | No | No |

| Costs drawable under r 42 | Yes | Yes | n/a | n/a | **No** | **No** |
| Written direction retained 7 years | - | Yes | - | Yes | Yes (the power) | Yes |

### 22.2 The ten things to get right

1. Deposit trust money to the GTA **as soon as practicable** after receipt.
2. Record everything in the cash books and ledger **within 5 working days**.
3. Reconciliation and trial balance **within 15 working days** of month end, started on day one.
4. **Never overdraw a client's ledger.** A debit balance is a breach even by $1, and an irregularity.
5. **Follow rule 42 exactly.** Bill referring to the proposed withdrawal, then 7 business days.
6. **Draw earned costs out promptly.** Leaving them in trust breaches s 146.
7. **Keep trust records 7 years**: receipts, ledgers, reconciliations, bank statements, client instructions.
8. **Use compliant software**, back it up off site, and **test the restore**.
9. **Use an authorised ADI** for the GTA.
10. **You are responsible**, however the day-to-day work is delegated.
